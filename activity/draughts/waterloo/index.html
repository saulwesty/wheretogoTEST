What is Draughts, Waterloo?

Draughts is a board game café and bar: you pay a gaming fee for a table, then play anything from a library of more than 1,000 games while ordering food and drink. Waterloo sits in a railway arch in the Leake Street graffiti tunnel, beneath Waterloo station.

There's a second Draughts in Stratford, at the Olympic Park.

How does it work?

You book a table and pay a per-person gaming fee, which gives you unlimited access to the library for up to three hours, subject to availability. Game Gurus on the floor will suggest a game that suits your group and teach you the rules.

It's one of the most family-friendly venues in this guide: under-12s play free, and teenagers get a reduced rate.

How much does Draughts, Waterloo cost?

£7.95 per adult off-peak or £9.95 at peak, for up to three hours of games.

Do kids pay at Draughts?

Under-12s play free; 13 to 17-year-olds pay £4 off-peak or £5 at peak.

What is the food and drink like?

A full kitchen runs alongside the games, with vegetarian, vegan and gluten-free options, plus a bar with beer, cocktails and Prosecco. Outside food and drink isn't allowed.

How do you get there?

Get directions on Google Maps ↗

Arch 16, Leake Street, SE1 7NN. Leake Street runs underneath Waterloo station, a couple of minutes from the exits, and close to the South Bank.

How do you book?

Book a table online through Draughts' own site; it's recommended at peak times and weekends, when tables sell out.
